**Project Overview**

The Jinzhou–Dayou Energy–Panjin oil pipeline is a critical energy artery of the New Northeast Land-Sea Corridor. Stretching 127 kilometers, the pipeline is lined with unattended stations and valve chambers. Located in a severe cold region, the project features multiple nodes, long-distance transmission, and an unattended operating model, placing extremely high demands on industrial-grade network reliability, ultra-low-temperature adaptability, and remote manageability and controllability. End-to-end communications was established as a prerequisite for project commissioning.

**Core Solution**

To address the communication challenges of the oil pipeline scenario—large scale, multiple nodes, and harsh environments—BDCOM built a fully industrial-grade private communication network architecture featuring three-layer aggregation and dual-network isolation. At the control center, a core switching platform was deployed, while the aggregation layer adopted a 10GE industrial ring network with dual-device hot standby, forming a highly reliable routing backbone. At the access layer, services are carried in separate zones based on business attributes. The production control system uses IES200 series industrial switches to build a self-healing ring network, ensuring millisecond-level switchover and zero packet loss for PLCs and data acquisition terminals. The security and auxiliary systems are independently carried by the IES200 PoE series ring network, where a single Ethernet cable delivers both data transmission and remote power supply, enabling simplified access for surveillance, public broadcasting, and fire protection terminals.

Across the entire network, industrial-grade wide-temperature devices and optical fiber media are used to support startup and operation at temperatures as low as -40°C and long-distance transmission up to 20 km. Through ring redundancy and hierarchical management and control, the solution creates a communications lifeline that keeps this unattended energy artery flowing without interruption, even in the event of network failures.
